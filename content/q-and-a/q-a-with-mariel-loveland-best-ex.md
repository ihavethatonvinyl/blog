Q&A is an occasional feature on IHTOV, where people answer a set of questions about their relationship with vinyl and music. Today we welcome Mariel Loveland of Best Ex



![](/images/upload/bestex.jpeg)

**Have you ever bought a record just for the artwork?** 

Strangely enough, no. My vinyl collection has been carefully curated. I tend to only buy my favorite albums or albums my friends make. It’s something I really treasure as this physical representation of everything I love. 

With CDs though? That’s where I throw caution to the wind. My favorite random cover art picks are *Earthquake Glue* by Guided by Voices and *Fuckin A* by the Thermals. The art was cool, and I was happy to discover the music totally matched. 




**What is your most memorable vinyl buying/receiving experience?** 

I remember back when I was touring a lot with Candy Hearts, at the start of every tour, I would pick one record to find. In our downtime, I’d visit record shops all across the US and search for that one record. I was super broke, but I always set aside enough money for that and that alone. I searched for *Majesty Shredding* by Superchunk for a year. I was delighted when I finally found it. Can’t remember where. Might have been Amoeba in Los Angeles. I wasdoubly as thrilled we got to play some festival with them later on. I always think of that record fondly. 




**What’s the most treasured album in your collection and why?** 

My most treasured album is *Loveless* by My Bloody Valentine. This was another record I spent months searching for, and ultimately could not find it. But a fan brought it to me when we toured in Europe. It’s one of the most thoughtful gifts someone has given me.




That record is ultra-special because I had it on my iPod on repeat in an 8 hour playlist. I would sleep to it every single night on tour. Every single night. I couldn’t sleep without it.




**What one record in your collection would you be most eager to share with new friends?**

The one record I always try to push on people is *Reconstruction Site* by the Weakerthans. They are my absolute favorite band of all time and criminally underrated. I genuinely have no idea how they weren’t the biggest rock band in the world. Like how is 30 Seconds To Mars selling out arenas but not The Weakerthans?




**Do you have a definitive album of choice for spring, summer, autumn and winter?**

I do think certain albums feel like different seasons but I never thought about it that much. Off the top of my head, here’s my list, but it probably has to do with when I first consistently overplayed these albums rather than what they actually sound like:

	- Winter: Dear You by Jawbreaker
	- Spring: Fountains of Wayne (Self Titled)
- Summer: Better Oblivion Community Center (Self Titled)
	- Fall: Congratulations I’m Sorry by the Gin Blossoms




**What is/are your white whale records, something you have your eye on but haven’t been able to get?**

Jets to Brazil - Perfecting Loneliness. It’s a perfect album. 




**Who/what got you hooked on records?**

This makes me so uncool to admit, but I want to say it was the music industry! I never had an interest in collecting vinyl until my band started making vinyl. I know my dad had this monster collection of hundreds of records that sat on shelves in our basement, but it felt like obsolete media at the time. It was all about CDs and burning mixtapes on that one computer that sat in your family room. Then everything got put on an iPod.

The guys in Candy Hearts were very into vinyl. When they were gushing over the pressing details of our first release with Bridge 9, I was like people still care about this? They buy this? Then I had to make the art for it and pick the colors and the design the etching. It really made me think, wow, this actually is an artform in itself. It gave me a whole new appreciation. 

I feel really grateful that I was pushed into seeing that because it just made my experience as a music fan so much richer. 




**What’s your favorite record to listen to on headphones?**

I love walking around my neighborhood listening to “It’s A Shame About Ray” and pretending I am a melancholy teen in 1995 trapped in a miserable New England winter. I don’t have to pretend that hard when it starts getting dark early.




**Tell us a little about your favorite record store**

I loved, with every fiber of my being, the Rough Trade that used to exist in Williamsburg. I was so upset when it closed. My experience in New York in the 2010s was extraordinarily like the show *Girls,* and Rough Trade felt like the center of all of it. It was that sort of Pitchfork era, on-the-pulse indie. The coolest bands would come through. It represented everything the neighborhood was at the time, before it essentially became a combination Hermes-Chanel-Lululemon. I actually think Lululemon may have even been priced out in favor of Alo.




I was looking to fill that hole for a long time. I’d say my favorite now is Limited to One in the East Village. It’s like a relic of the New York I loved. I was absolutely blessed to have discovered this gem through my label. Kristian the owner truly loves music and has a knowledge about punk and indie that goes so unbelievably deep. I could go there and listen to his picks for hours. I was absolutely blessed to host my record release show there.




**What’s the weirdest record you own?** 

I don’t think I own anything weird. The weirdest stuff you’ll find are records from my ex boyfriends, but I like to keep everything I’ve been a part of, even if it was just emotional support.  I do recall having a CD of Halloween sounds when I was a kid. We would point the stereo outside of an open window and play it all Halloween! 




**Have you ever bought a record based on hearing it playing in a record store?**

I used to actually go to Borders Books and Virgin all the time and pick random records to listen to. If I liked the previews of whatever song those little preview headphones would let me hear, I’d buy it. I miss that a lot. 




**How has your record collection and appreciation for vinyl evolved over the years, and what has influenced your tastes?**

I think I explained that above! I don’t know if vinyl has influenced my tastes, but I do appreciate when artists get the whole vision right with the art and packaging. It really adds to my enjoyment of the sound. 




**Name the top three records you own that could describe you or your vibe**

Oh this is so tough. I mostly only buy vinyl from 90s alternative bands or DIY artists I see at shows. I don’t tend to buy pop records. I think the three that describe me are: Reunion Tour by the Weakerthans; Lifted by Bright Eyes; and It’s a Shame About Ray by the Lemonheads (I know I mentioned this one before but it’s one of my favorite albums of all time). 




**What’s the last record you played?**

My own record. I just got my hands on it last week!

*Best Ex is the solo project of New Jersey singer-songwriter Mariel Loveland, who previously fronted pop punk band Candy Hearts, sharing stages with the likes of Man Overboard, New Found Glory, and Weezer. She launched Best Ex in 2017, jumping headfirst into a full U.S. tour to support her EP Ice Cream Anti-Social. Since then, Loveland has released the EP Good At Feeling Bad(2020) and debut full-length, With a Smile(2023) —music that blends diary-like introspection and biting sarcasm, sitting at the edge of bedroom synthpop and high-gloss production. Her writing has earned acclaim from Billboard, Alternative Press, and Kerrang, with airplay on BBC Radio 1, BBC Radio 6, and Idobi Radio. Good Girl(Iodine Recordings) marks Loveland’s return to guitars, trading synths for 90s-inspired sheen, without losing an ounce of her whip-smart lyricism.*
