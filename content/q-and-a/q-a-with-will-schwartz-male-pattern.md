Q&A is an occasional feature on IHTOV, where people answer a set of questions about their relationship with vinyl and music. Today we welcome Will Schwartz, who performs under the name Male Pattern







**What is your most memorable vinyl buying/receiving experience?** 

My first records came from my mom. She was a hippy - lived in a commune in Big Sur in the late 60s, slummed it on the Haight in the summer of love - her stories from the time period are hilarious and charming, went to Dead acid tests. She gave me what was left of her collection from when she was a teenager - mostly Rolling Stones and Jimi Hendrix. Her name is Sue, and in a misguided and very dated attempt at self-mystification and cultural appropriation, had rechristened herself Sioux, at the time. All of her records were stamped with her name - Sioux. It’s hilarious and charming to me, to get this relic of that time, and she couldn’t possibly be more different now.

**What’s the most treasured album in your collection and why?** 

It’s probably my hand-me-down “Axis: Bold as Love” - it’s the original from the 60s and the record opens up with a tri-panel hallucinogenic art-piece with Jimi and his band immersed among different Hindu gods. It rips.

**What one record in your collection would you be most eager to share with new friends?**

Assuming these new friends don’t know it already, I would play the Silver Jews’ “American Water.” I’ve had the record for years, since the early aughts, and the opening bars are so grabby and awesome (“In 1984 I was hospitalized for approaching perfection). The record sounds great on a good sound system. It’s one of my favorites.

**Do you have a definitive album of choice for spring, summer, autumn and winter?**

I think winter has a very distinct listening vibe. I like listening to Bach in the winter - Gould is my favorite interpreter - I particularly love the partitas, but I also have Edward Aldwell's record of Well Tempered Clavier. Van Karajen conducting St. Matthew Passion. These are all winter staples to me. I love baroque music in general - Gould’s Handel solo piano are unstoppable. Other than that, it’s anything goes.

**What is/are your white whale records, something you have your eye on but haven’t been able to get?**

I would love an original print of Oh Moicy by Michael Hurley, Jeff Friedrichs, and the Unholy Modal Rounders. I think it’s on Rounder records. I love Hurley’s art. Any of his records would be welcome in my collection and I don’t have any.

**Who/what got you hooked on records?**

I think I saw the movie High Fidelity with John Cusack when I was a kid and his apartment blew me away - that you could be surrounded by music like that. My parents also collected records, or had some solid collections of their own.

**What’s your favorite record to listen to on headphones?**

It’s fun to listen to opera on headphones - to really immerse yourself, read the booklet that comes along with the set. I’ll say *Tosca.* I have one that Solti conducted that’s great.

**Tell us a little about your favorite record store**

I like thrift stores and yard sales. Anything where you can see what the original collection it came from looked like - who that listener was, what they liked, the oddities and outliers that populate the collection that make them more intriguing. But I’ve never struck out at Academy in NYC. Our local record shop in Queens is called Stray. It’s very well curated.

**What’s the weirdest record you own?** 

I don’t know about weird, but the record that stuck out to me and that most blew my mind when I got it was Jelly Roll Morton in conversation with Alan Lomax. These recordings have been put out in a lot of forms - notably by the Smithsonian. But this was a very old record - it was part of some kind of mail order series from the 50s or early 60s. The record is Jelly Roll talking to Lomax, telling stories about pimping and crime and life in New Orleans and playing songs from different periods of his career and singing in a wonderful warm baritone. This record had "Winin’ Boy Blues” on it and a number of his more pornographic and lurid songs. It shocked me when I heard it, and it cracked me up. I didn’t know people made things like that back then. Since then, I’ve heard the whole run of recordings from that session, and they’re very diverse and interesting - the blue stuff is really relegated to about an hour of play.

**How has your record collection and appreciation for vinyl evolved over the years, and what has influenced your tastes?**

I used to be snobby about newly pressed vinyl - I wanted collectibles. Now I’ll buy any edition of anything, as long as it plays well.

**Name the top three records you own that could describe you or your vibe**

This might be aspirational but….

 Archie Schepp - *Mama Too Tight*
Glenn Gould - Handel’s *Suites for Harpsichord*
Pavement - *Wowee Zowee*

**What’s the last record you played?**



Sonny Rollins - “Saxophone Colossus” is on the turntable.



* **Will Schwartz** (he/him), is a songwriter, novelist, and English professor from Queens, NY, who performs under the name The Male Pattern.*

*His first musical work in over a decade, ‘I Was Shot in the Heart!’ is a character-driven, narrative work that explores and exploits Americana tropes to capture its time and place. It’s a [concept album](https://youtu.be/kLGOlZe4Vjc?si=LYI7uRUdoIHja28A) in the tradition of **Darren Hayman’s** ‘Pram Town’ (dubbed a “[Folk Opera](https://darrenhayman.bandcamp.com/album/pram-town-a-folk-opera)”), **Terry Allen’s** ‘Lubbock (On Everything),’ and **Alex Cameron’s** ‘Forced Witness.’*
